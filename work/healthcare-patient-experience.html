Designing AI Assisted Experiences for Preventive Healthcare

Designed a consumer diagnostic experience helping users discover relevant tests, understand reports, and track long-term health patterns using contextual AI.

RoleProduct Designer
IndustryHealthcare Diagnostics
Duration6 Months

The Challenge

Diagnostic apps often provide large amounts of information but limited guidance.

Users struggled to:

  • Identify relevant tests
  • Understand reports
  • Know when action was needed
  • Trust recommendations that felt promotional

The challenge was designing AI that informed users without overwhelming them.

Choosing the Right Tests

Most diagnostic apps present long catalogs of tests without context. Users had to know what they needed before they arrived — which assumed medical literacy most people don't have.

We designed a recommendation layer that surfaces relevant tests based on symptoms, age, past results, and health history. Each suggestion includes a brief, plain-language explanation of why it might matter — not a prescription, but a starting point for informed decisions.

Key decisions

  • Symptom-first entry: Users describe what they feel, not what test they need.
  • Profile-aware packages: Recommendations adapt to age, gender, and health history.
  • Plain-language rationale: Every suggestion explains why — building context, not just offering a catalog.

Understandable Reports

Lab reports are one of the most common outputs of healthcare — and one of the least understood. Traditional diagnostic PDFs present dense numerical worksheets with no interpretation layer, forcing users to wait for a doctor visit to understand what their results mean.

We redesigned the report experience as a layered summary: a top-level status card with natural-language interpretation, followed by trend visualizations that compare current values against the user's own history. Users can drill into clinical detail when ready — but the default view prioritizes clarity.

Key decisions

  • Status-first summaries: Lead with plain-language interpretation, not raw numbers.
  • Longitudinal sparklines: Multi-quarter trend views help users see patterns across time.
  • Progressive disclosure: Clinical detail is available on demand, not front-loaded.

"Recent cholesterol levels are moving toward the upper range. Consider discussing dietary adjustments with your physician."

Explainability & Trust

Healthcare is an emotional environment. AI recommendations in this space carry a different weight than product suggestions — they directly affect how a person feels about their own body. If the reasoning behind a suggestion isn't transparent, it creates anxiety rather than reducing it.

We built an explainability layer into every AI-generated insight. Each recommendation answers four questions: Why is this relevant? What signal triggered it? What has changed? How does it connect to my history? The goal was not to make users trust the AI — but to give them enough information to trust their own judgment.

Explainability framework

  • Why? Contextual rationale tied to the user's health profile.
  • What signal? The specific data point or trend that triggered the insight.
  • What changed? Comparison against previous results to highlight movement.
  • Relevance? How the insight connects to the user's broader health history.

AI Design Principles

These principles guided every AI-driven interaction across the product. They were established early in the process and referenced throughout design reviews.

Clarity Over Intelligence
If the AI output can't be understood in one read, it shouldn't ship.
Confidence, Not Compliance
The AI should increase the user's confidence in their own decisions, not replace them.
Emotional Sensitivity
Health data is personal. Tone, framing, and timing all carry clinical weight.
Explain, Don't Persuade
Every recommendation must be traceable, transparent, and opt-in.

Reflection

Healthcare experiences are emotional environments. Designing AI for this space required more restraint than ambition — the most effective interventions were the ones that reduced noise, not added features.

The biggest shift in this project wasn't technical. It was reframing AI's role from "intelligent assistant" to "contextual guide" — something that helps users build understanding rather than dependence. In diagnostics, the measure of good design isn't engagement. It's whether someone leaves the app feeling more informed and less anxious than when they arrived.
