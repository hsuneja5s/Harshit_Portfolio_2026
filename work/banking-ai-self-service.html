AI banking assistant — turning AI hesitation into self-service

Designed a conversational banking assistant that resolves blank-input anxiety through structured quick-action chips and context-aware transaction cards.

RoleSenior Product Designer
TeamPwC India
TimelineMar 2024 — Dec 2024

The Challenge & Insights

Blank chat inputs in banking apps trigger cognitive freeze. When presented with an empty prompt, 70% of users abandon the interface, converting potential self-service opportunities into expensive call-center tickets. We designed a context-aware assistant that guides users from friction to transaction.

Core friction points:

  • Prompt Anxiety: Blank conversational text areas create cognitive load for users who do not know what tasks are possible.
  • Paragraph Fatigue: Reading long text instructions in a chat thread makes account management slow and confusing.
  • Regulatory Bounds: Users hesitate to trust conversational bots with financial transactions unless guardrails are explicit.
Prompt Friction

"I open the chat and just stare at it. I don't even know what I'm allowed to ask."

Cognitive Load

"Don't explain how to transfer money in a paragraph. Just let me do it right here."

Trust & Bounds

"Is this bot secure? Can it accidentally send my money to the wrong person?"

Design Goal

Position conversational banking as a high-fidelity transaction cockpit—shifting conversational query volume into instant, structured self-service.

Guide
Bypass blank-input freeze with high-frequency action chips.
Structure
Replace text-heavy dialogues with interactive payment widgets.
Protect
Define strict regulatory guardrails to build absolute trust.
Escalate
Ensure zero-friction warm handoffs to live bank support agents.

Key Experiences

We structured the AI assistant as a widget-driven cockpit, serving actions first and resolving entries dynamically.

01 / Action Menu: Quick-action chips bypass the blank chat input freeze, offering immediate tap-targets.
02 / Transaction Cockpit: Structured widgets and rich interactive cards render ledgers directly on-canvas.
03 / Intent Guidance: Multi-step transaction cards guide transfers and billing payments within the conversational stream.

AI design principles applied:

Action Over Typing
Provide clear, contextual tap-targets instead of demanding open-ended prompt typing.
Widget-First Resolution
Display structured micro-applications on-canvas rather than typing long textual steps.
Proactive Deflection
Analyze recent banking actions to surface contextual support triggers instantly.
Explicit Boundaries
Ensure users immediately know which complex operational and financial tasks require human handoff.

Refusal matrix

In highly regulated retail banking, trust is built on transparency. To ensure compliance, we designed an explicit refusal and warm handoff framework that maps out exactly what the assistant will and won't execute.

Intent routing guardrails:

  • Intents Handled (Self-Serve): Account balance check, transaction ledger display, quick transfer, billing pay, and debit card blocking.
  • Intents Refused (Handoff): Financial planning, credit-line advice, payment disputes, and loan/mortgage applications.
  • Warm Escalation: Immediate, secure context transfer to a live support agent when high-risk boundaries are touched.
Safe AI fallback interface with instant live agent link.

Reflection

Conversational banking fails when it forces users to type. By shifting the paradigm from a "text-based chatbot" to a "guided visual cockpit," we bypassed prompt anxiety entirely. In high-trust environments, the best conversational assistant is the one that gives users structured control rather than open-ended choices.
