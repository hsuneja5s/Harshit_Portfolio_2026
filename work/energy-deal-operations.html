Deal intelligence for global energy operations

A dashboard reports the past. A cockpit lets the pilot fly.

A unified control hub—The Pacific Platform—that consolidated multiple separate trackers and hundreds of active regional deals into a single, workflow-aligned interface, shifting finance from manual reconciliation to active performance management.

RoleSenior Product Designer
ClientGlobal energy enterprise
TimelineJan 2025 — Jun 2025

In brief

  • The problem was fragmented tracking, not reporting. Finance leaders were forced to stitch time bookings, deal details, and budget allocations across spreadsheets and legacy tools.
  • The bet: Design a single, workflow-aligned landing hub (The Pacific Platform) with tabbed metadata detail and an active risk layer directly embedded where budgets live.
  • The shift: Shifted reviewing effort from offline slide-pack assembly to dynamic in-flight checks, surfacing burn-rate risks weeks before month-end reviews.

The story

The finance function at a global energy enterprise oversees a highly complex portfolio of regional deals (e.g., digital infrastructure, regional partnerships), but deal data, time bookings, and budget information lived in separate tools.

Answering basic questions such as which deals are active, where budgets stand, and whether capacity is constrained required hours of manual data assembly. Review preparation relied on offline slides instead of a single, trusted cockpit.

The bet

We reframed the problem from "another dashboard" to a centralized landing cockpit called **The Pacific Platform**. We made one explicit call: **bring active deals, resources, and budget utilization together in a tabbed, high-density interface.**

Instead of building heavy analytical scenario builders, we focused on legibility: structuring key metadata (Decision Executives, IM Advisors, Country) and opportunity status checklists directly on the deal card, so review teams could confirm WBS creation and data compliance in a single scan.

The cockpit

The landing hub structures active portfolios and unifies workflows, bringing four core views into a clean, tabbed interface:

Pacific Home
Central hub with standardized quick actions: Submit Deal to Prioritisation Board, Add a Deal, and Open Existing Deal.
Opportunity Details
Structured metadata mapping Decision Executives, IM Advisors, Line of Business, and Strategic themes (e.g., Integrated Gas, LNG).
Opportunity Status
Compliance checklist tracking Mandatory Data, SharePoint Site Setup, and WBS creation.
Budget Cockpit
Planned versus actual spend, category breakdown (FM Staff hours), and contextual AI spend forecasting.

The deal view organizes data into clear tabs: Details, Contributors, Budget (with a status dot), and Highlights. Rather than burying compliance, the status panel actively guides reviewers to SharePoint configurations and WBS statuses. Data inputs are kept lightweight so deal owners can maintain current statuses without administrative friction.

Insight.AI

The Budget tab embeds **Insight.AI**, an active risk layer designed to flag spend trajectory anomalies proactively where budget reviews occur.

  • Contextual AI Warnings: Restrained, high-visibility warning blocks alerting teams directly: "Burn rate has increased by 18% this month. At this pace, budget exhaustion is projected 3 weeks before deal closure."
  • Granular Category breakdown: Maps costs by category (FM Staff actual hours and costs) next to a clean monthly spend trend bar chart.
  • Longitudinal spend trends: Displays multi-year trajectories at a glance, allowing immediate context checks against historical averages.

In high-stakes enterprise applications, transparency builds adoption. Insight.AI works contextually, placing explanation metrics alongside actual category bookings so finance teams can verify the forecast instantly.

Business outcome

0 hrs
Manual spreadsheet assembly. Review prep collapsed from hours of stitching offline files to a single, trusted live cockpit.
Consolidated
Active deals managed in one shared system instead of multiple separate legacy trackers, aligning finance, business, and technology.
In-flight
Visibility of performance, budget, and capacity issues, enabling early intervention before month-end reviews.

What we didn't ship

Every deliberate cut bought the cockpit a better chance of actually being used.

  • Complex scenario modeling. We avoided adding heavy simulation tools in v1 to prioritize platform legibility. Establishing a single source of truth for active deals took precedence.
  • Granular custom report builders. Held the line on a standardized, shared vocabulary. Custom review views were deferred to v2 to prevent fragmentation of review standards.
  • Full contributor writing permissions. The initial release prioritized the leader-out review cockpit, keeping data entry fields highly focused and lightweight.

With structured deal data and workflows in one place, the platform serves as a solid foundation for future integrations, including trend projections across quarters and expanded AI-driven indicators of deal health.
