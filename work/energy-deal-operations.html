Problem

By the time a number reached a slide, it was already a week old.

Regional deals each lived in their own tracker. Preparing a single review meant exporting every one, stitching them into a master sheet, chasing owners for missing updates, and recomputing burn by hand before anything could be presented. The pipeline was real, but it was never current.

Solution

The Pacific Platform: a deal cockpit finance can trust.

One control hub that consolidates hundreds of regional deals into a single, workflow aligned interface, with budget, status, and compliance living on the same card.

The measure of success was not a feature list. It was a finance leader opening one screen and, for the first time, trusting the number they saw.

Design Decisions

We identified the most legible way to bring the pipeline to life.

The old process was not a workflow, it was a reconciliation ritual repeated every cycle. The design goal was blunt: remove every step a system could do for a human.

Before / After
Before
The reconciliation ritual
  • Export each region's tracker by hand
  • Stitch them into a master spreadsheet
  • Chase deal owners for missing updates
  • Recompute burn and margin manually
  • Rebuild the review slide deck
  • Circulate, then repeat next cycle
After
The live cockpit
  • Open the cockpit to a live pipeline
  • Read burn, budget, and risk in place
  • Act on the deal that is slipping
Systems thinking
Information architecture

Information architecture came out of a stakeholder workshop that mapped the deal lifecycle end to end. We explored three cockpit models: a lightweight summary, a deep single deal workspace, and a high density pipeline with metadata and compliance on the primary cards. We bet on the third. In enterprise finance, the cost of a second click is a lost thread, so legibility beats minimalism.

Testing an early split layout, users kept losing the thread between a deal and its money. The fix was not more screens, it was fewer: budget, status, and compliance had to live on the same card.

Reflection

What I learned.

Density is a feature.
In enterprise finance, a well organised dense view beats a minimal one. Users came to scan, not to explore, and the layout had to respect that.
Trust is the real deliverable.
The platform did not win because it had more features. It won because a leader could open one screen and believe the number without reconciling it first.
What we didn't ship
  • Complex scenario modeling. Deferred so phase one could nail a single source of truth first.
  • Custom report builders. Standardised the review taxonomy to keep every dashboard reading the same way.
  • Full editing permissions. Capped inputs in v1 to keep data entry lightweight and the pipeline trustworthy.
