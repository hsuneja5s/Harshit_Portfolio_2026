Deal intelligence for global energy operations

A dashboard reports the past. A cockpit lets the pilot fly.

A unified deal management cockpit that consolidated multiple separate trackers and hundreds of active deals into a single, workflow-aligned interface — shifting the finance function's effort from manual reconciliation to real-time performance management.

RoleSenior Product Designer
ClientGlobal energy enterprise
TimelineJan 2025 — Jun 2025

In brief

  • The problem wasn't reporting — it was time-to-decision. Finance leaders were learning about deal-level risk weeks after it became actionable due to fragmented offline tracking.
  • The bet: build a single, workflow-aligned control cockpit that brings active deals, resources, and budgets together so finance can act, not just report.
  • The shift: consolidated hundreds of active deals from multiple separate trackers into a single shared system, reducing reviewer spreadsheet assembly time to zero.

The story

The finance function at a global energy enterprise oversees a large portfolio of deals across businesses and regions, but deal data, time bookings, and budget information lived in multiple spreadsheets and legacy tools. This fragmentation made it impossible to get a reliable, end-to-end view.

Answering basic questions such as which deals are at risk, where budgets are off, and where capacity is constrained required hours of manual data stitching. As a result, month-end reviews depended on slide packs and offline files instead of a single, trusted cockpit.

The bet

We reframed the problem from "another dashboard" to a control cockpit for finance. We made one explicit call early: the risk and allocation layers had to live inside the day-to-day deal workflow, not next to it. A standalone analytics tool would have shipped faster and demoed beautifully, but it would have failed adoption. Finance leaders don't want a fifth tool to look at — they want one interface that eliminates the friction of the other four.

We deliberately sacrificed forecasting modules, scenario sandboxes, and granular custom builders in v1. Every one of those was on a stakeholder wishlist, but we deferred them. We bet entirely on legibility: making the cockpit so intuitive that a leader opening it cold could identify exception-level risks within seconds.

The cockpit

The tool brings four core views into a single, workflow-aligned interface, eliminating the need for offline slide decks:

Deal Management
Structured records with status, value, key dates, and owners. Portfolio at a glance, drillable to a single deal.
Financial Dashboard
Portfolio KPIs with real-time drill-down into individual deal structures.
Resources & Time
Who's on which deal, booked time, and capacity hot spots.
Budget Utilization
Planned versus actual spend, with clear exception signals for over- and underspend.

Navigation is exception-first. Filters like "at risk", "over budget", and "capacity constrained" are prominent status actions, not buried selectors. The design forces focus: leaders open the platform to find the few deals that need intervention this week, not to scan the hundreds that don't. Data inputs are kept extremely lean so analysts can keep records current without heavy administrative friction.

Risk layer

The budget view embeds an active risk layer. It proactively answers the core question finance teams ask every week: "is this deal off track, and how much warning do I have?"

  • Burn-rate alerts: "Burn rate up in recent weeks. Budget exhaustion projected ahead of schedule." Surfaces during the quarter, not after.
  • Cost-by-category anomalies: flags categories where actual spend diverges sharply from planned allocations, with comparison windows cited inline.
  • Spend-trend forward views: projects FY trajectories from current actuals, citing the historical spend comparison it's anchored on.

In a corporate finance context, trust comes from verification, not blind faith. Every indicator has a "why this" affordance surfacing the underlying calculation and comparison window. That design call is why the features passed internal risk and audit reviews on the first cycle, avoiding manual approval gates.

Business outcome

0 hrs
Manual spreadsheet assembly. Review prep collapsed from hours of stitching offline files to a single, trusted live cockpit.
Consolidated
Active deals managed in one shared system instead of multiple separate legacy trackers, aligning finance, business, and technology.
In-flight
Visibility of performance, budget, and capacity issues, enabling early intervention before month-end reviews.

What we didn't ship

Every deliberate cut bought the cockpit a better chance of actually being used.

  • Forecasting and scenario analysis. The instinct was to add it in v1 to make the platform feel "complete." We resisted because adoption of the core cockpit had to land first — forecasting on top of untrusted data is worse than no forecasting at all.
  • Custom report builders for BU finance leads. Every business unit wanted its own slice of the data. We held the line on a shared vocabulary because custom layouts before shared understanding just fragments reviews. Acknowledged debt for v2.
  • Contributor experience for deal owners. The cockpit was designed leader-out, meaning deal owners got a thinner experience by design. Their input flows lagged by a full release cycle. If I were starting again, I'd parallel-track contributors from day one.

With structured deal data and workflows in one place, the finance function now has a platform for scenario analysis, trend views across quarters, and future integrations with forecasting and AI-driven indicators of deal health.
