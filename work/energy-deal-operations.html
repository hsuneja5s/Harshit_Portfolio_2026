01 · Who, why & what

Before designing a single screen, I sat with the people who lived in the spreadsheets. Across [ NN ] interviews with regional deal leads, finance controllers, and portfolio managers, the same story surfaced: the data existed, but it was never in one place at one time, so nobody trusted it until it had been manually reconciled.

What we heard, in their words:

Fragmentation

"I rebuild the same master sheet every week before I can even start reviewing."

Trust

"By the time the numbers reach a slide, they are already a week old."

Risk, too late

"We find the budget overrun at quarter close, not while we can still act on it."

Three people, three jobs, one shared pipeline:

The Deal Lead
Regional, owns the opportunity

Needs to log time and budget against a deal without spreadsheet gymnastics, and to know exactly which compliance gate is blocking progress.

Frustration Status lives in their head and three tabs. Updates are a chore, so they slip.

The Finance Controller
Reconciles budget against actuals

Needs a single source of truth for planned versus actual cost across every region, with burn visible as it happens.

Frustration Spends more time stitching data than interpreting it.

The Portfolio Manager
Executive, reads the whole book

Needs burn and compliance trends across all regions at a glance, and a way to spot the deal that is quietly slipping.

Frustration Sees a curated snapshot, never the live pipeline.

We organised every user story into six jobs to be done, which became the spine of the console:

Create
Open a new deal and frame the opportunity in minutes.
Track
Log time and budget against a live deal as work happens.
Reconcile
See planned versus actual cost in one place, no stitching.
Forecast
Read burn rate and project the runway before it bites.
Audit
Trace every gate clearance and edit through the activity log.
Report
Surface portfolio trends without rebuilding a slide.

The scope we committed to: consolidate the regional trackers into one pipeline, put compliance and budget on the primary deal cards, and give leaders a live read of burn instead of a retrospective one. Everything that did not serve that single source of truth was deferred.

02 · The foundational flow & IA

The old process was not a workflow, it was a reconciliation ritual repeated every cycle. The design goal for the flow was blunt: remove every step a system could do for a human.

Before
The reconciliation ritual
  • Export each region's tracker by hand
  • Stitch them into a master spreadsheet
  • Chase deal owners for missing updates
  • Recompute burn and margin manually
  • Rebuild the review slide deck
  • Circulate, then repeat next cycle
After
The live cockpit
  • Open the cockpit to a live pipeline
  • Read burn, budget, and risk in place
  • Act on the deal that is slipping

Information architecture came out of a stakeholder workshop that mapped the deal lifecycle end to end. The pipeline resolved into five workflow aligned views, each answering one question a finance leader actually asks, so the navigation mirrored the job rather than the database.

The budget cockpit. Planned versus actual, live, with burn rate alerts on the card.

Testing an early split layout, users kept losing the thread between a deal and its money. The fix was not more screens, it was fewer: budget, status, and compliance had to live on the same card.

03 · Design evolution

We explored three models for the cockpit: a lightweight summary view, a deep single deal workspace, and a high density pipeline where metadata and compliance sit directly on the primary cards. We bet on the third. In an enterprise finance context, the cost of a second click is a lost thread, so legibility and scanning beat minimalism.

That bet only worked if the components could carry the density without becoming noise. Status indicators, buttons, and tables were rebuilt for scannability, then contributed back into the platform's design system so every future view inherited the same clarity.

The pipeline organised into five workflow aligned views:

01 / Budget Cockpit: Live actual versus planned cost tracking with burn rate alerts.
02 / Detailed View: Core opportunity details and metadata in one place.
03 / Historic Dashboard: Historical margins and status compliance pipelines.
04 / Activity Log: Audit trails tracking every gate clearance and edit.
05 / Portfolio Manager View: Executive dashboard with burn and compliance trends.

Insight.AI

A live cockpit answers "what is happening." Finance leaders also ask "what should I do about it." So we built Insight.AI directly into the platform, a conversational layer that turns the dashboard into a dialogue.

  • Conversational guidance: leaders query burn rates, budgets, and forecasts in plain language, no pivot table required.
  • Proactive risk checks: the assistant flags anomalies and surfaces the deal at risk before it reaches quarter close.
  • Grounded, not guessing: every answer traces back to a real number in the pipeline, so trust is earned, not assumed.
Insight.AI. Deal performance and prediction, in plain language.

What we didn't ship

  • Complex scenario modeling. Deferred so phase one could nail a single source of truth first.
  • Custom report builders. Standardised the review taxonomy to keep every dashboard reading the same way.
  • Full editing permissions. Capped inputs in v1 to keep data entry lightweight and the pipeline trustworthy.
